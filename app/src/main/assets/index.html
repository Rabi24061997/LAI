Both modes measure the gap fraction P: the share of sky seen through the canopy from below. Beer–Lambert's law links P to leaf area:

P(θ) = exp( −G(θ) · LAI / cos θ )

θ is the view zenith angle and G is the leaf projection function. Sky and leaf pixels are separated on the blue channel using Otsu's automatic threshold, unless you set a manual one.

Normal mode (single angle)

At θ = 57.5°, G ≈ 0.5 for any leaf angle distribution, so:

LAI = −ln(P) · cos 57.5° / 0.5 ≈ −1.075 · ln(P)

P is measured in the centre window of the photo (about ±10° around the camera axis).

Fisheye mode (five rings, LI-COR style)

With a 180° lens, distance from the image centre maps to zenith angle (θ = 90° · r / R). The image is split into the same five rings used by the LI-COR LAI-2200, and LAI is the weighted sum:

LAI = 2 · Σ Wi · cos θi · ( −ln Pi )

Plot summary

  • Mean and SD are given separately for each mode.
  • Normal mode also shows the Lang–Xiang clumping estimate, ln(mean P) / mean(ln P).
  • Fisheye mode also shows a LI-COR-style value: ring gap fractions are averaged over all fisheye readings first, then converted to LAI. This is the closest match to how the LAI-2200 combines its below readings.

Limits

  • Values are effective LAI and include stems and branches. True LAI needs a clumping correction.
  • Readings above about LAI 5 saturate.
  • Best light: uniform overcast sky, or soon after sunrise or before sunset. Keep the sun out of the frame.
